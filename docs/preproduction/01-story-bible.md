# 01 — Story Bible

> **Legend.** **[FACT]** = established by the original concept and not changed. **[PROPOSAL]** = a creative suggestion awaiting approval. **[SCIENCE]** = grounded in real planetary science. **[FICTION]** = invented technology or biology, labelled so it is never presented as real.

---

## 1. Final game concept

**JUPITER: THE LAST TRANSMISSION** is a ~60-minute first-person survival-horror narrative game set in 2183 **[FACT]**. A five-person crew leaves Earth on the first crewed expedition to Europa. The official mission is geophysics. The real mission, known only to the commander, is to locate the source of a structured signal detected beneath the ice twelve years earlier, and to bring a living sample home **[PROPOSAL]**.

**Design pillar — "In vacuum, you hear through your feet."** **[SCIENCE + PROPOSAL]**
Europa has no meaningful atmosphere, so sound cannot travel through air on the surface. Outside, the player hears only what reaches them through their suit, through their boots on the ice, and over the radio. Creatures outside are therefore *silent* unless they touch the ice you are standing on. Inside pressurised spaces, sound returns, and so do the creatures' voices. This single rule drives the horror, the stealth and the sound design, and it keeps the game scientifically honest.

**Emotional pillars [FACT]:** attachment to Earth → attachment to the crew → insignificance before Jupiter → discomfort on Europa → fear → devastation → relief mixed with grief.

## 2. Story synopsis

**[FACT spine, PROPOSAL detail]**

In 2171 the robotic lander *Tiamat* melted a borehole into Europa's ice near Conamara Chaos. Eleven days later its seismometers recorded a low, rhythmic pulse rising from the ocean below. The pulse was not geological. It repeated with structure. Then *Tiamat* went silent. The data was classified under the designation **Feature 7**.

Twelve years later, the joint-agency expedition **Halcyon** launches from Earth aboard the deep-space vessel **Meridian**. The public is told it is studying Europa's ice shell and ocean. Only **Commander Daniel Ashford** has been briefed on Feature 7 and on a sealed secondary objective: retrieve a living sample.

Geophysicist **Dr. Sam Okafor** (the player) joined the crew late, replacing an engineer who was well liked. Flight engineer **Mara Lindqvist** never forgave him for it. Over eleven months of transit, through arguments, small kindnesses and a shared sense of humour, the two build a guarded respect.

At Jupiter, the surface seismometers left by *Tiamat* reawaken. Sonified through the ship's speakers, they carry a deep, slow vibration: something enormous moving under the ice. Soon the ship's own radar soundings begin coming back *changed*. Each pulse returns slightly reshaped, as though something below is answering. Communications fail and recover. Instruments read events that did not happen.

The crew land at Conamara Base, a habitat pre-built by robotic cargo landers. The ice is beautiful. Then they find *Tiamat*'s borehole: sealed from below by a growth that looks exactly like frost but pulses with light. Sam finds Ashford's sealed orders and understands that the radar he has been running, at Ashford's insistence, is what has been calling to it.

The organism's "forms" come up through the bore. Pilot **Luca Ferreira** dies at the lander, his last moments heard over the radio. **Dr. Hana Mori** is lost in the drill chamber. Ashford, trapped and dying, admits the truth over a failing channel and gives Sam the launch codes for the ascent vehicle.

Sam and Mara fight their way across the ice to the ascent vehicle. In the drill chamber, the tall predator Mara names **the Listener** corners Sam. Mara kills it, or appears to. As Sam thanks her, it rises again behind her. Her last words are part of a joke they have shared since training. Sam leaves alone.

On the *Meridian*, Sam discovers that Ashford had already secured a sample in the ascent vehicle's cargo. He must choose whether to bring it home or vent it to space, and he records the mission's final transmission to Earth.

Months later Sam comes home. The ending is quiet: Earth, family, and the empty space where five people should be standing. Something about Europa remains unexplained.

## 7. The central mystery and its explanation

### 7.1 What the player gradually learns

1. **Ch. 3:** A sound exists. It comes from beneath the ice. (Sonified seismometer data.)
2. **Ch. 4:** The sound responds to the ship. Radar pulses come back reshaped, echoing the ship's own rhythm.
3. **Ch. 5:** Something has been on the surface. Equipment is moved and wrapped in "frost" that should not form there.
4. **Ch. 6:** *Tiamat* was here in 2171 and detected this. The mission was sent *because* of it. Ashford's orders: obtain a living sample.
5. **Ch. 7:** The "frost" is alive. The creatures are not separate animals; they are parts of one thing.
6. **Ch. 9 (Hana's notebook and audio log):** Her final hypothesis is that the ocean holds a single distributed organism, an ecosystem behaving like a body. It grows specialised "forms" when wounded. *Tiamat*'s drill was a wound. The radar was the second.
7. **Ch. 10 (left open):** Was it attacking, or defending? Was the pulse a warning, or a voice? The game never says.

### 7.2 The explanation behind the scenes (the developer's truth)

**[FICTION, science-inspired]** Europa's ocean contains a vast, slow, colonial organism, a distributed network of cells spread through the ocean and along the underside of the ice shell. It has no brain. It communicates and coordinates through low-frequency mechanical pulses carried through the ice (**Feature 7**), and it is sensitive to strong electromagnetic and acoustic signals. **[SCIENCE]** Europa has an induced magnetic field generated by its salty, conductive ocean. **[FICTION]** The organism can slightly modulate that conductivity, which is how it produces the radio interference in Chapter 4.

When the ice is breached, the colony responds as an immune system would. It grows **forms**: specialised, short-lived bodies that seal breaches, sense intrusions and remove threats. Each form is pressurised internally and can survive the surface vacuum and cold only for limited periods.

*Tiamat*'s drill was the first injury. The *Meridian*'s repeated radar soundings, which Ashford ordered to locate the colony's "centre", were perceived as a sustained attack, so the colony mobilised. The crew are not being hunted by monsters; they are being treated as an infection.

This explanation is **never delivered in full**. Hana's notebook gets the player about 70% of the way there.

### 7.3 Why the mission's official story is incomplete

The sample objective was kept from the crew because the agencies feared a refusal and a public reaction. Ashford agreed because he believed a crew that didn't know would act more naturally around the "geophysics" cover story, and because he wanted to be the first human to make contact. His tragedy is that he was right about the discovery and wrong about everything else.

## 8. Creature biology and designs

All forms share one **visual language** so they read as one ecosystem: translucent blue-grey hide stretched tight by internal pressure; dark organs visible behind it; frost crystals forming on any exposed surface; bioluminescent **pressure nodes** (cold cyan) that pulse in time with Feature 7. When wounded, the hide vents pressurised fluid that **flash-freezes into black-red crystalline spray** in vacuum. This is gore with a scientific reason.

### 8.1 The Listener — primary predator (signature creature)

| Aspect | Design |
|---|---|
| **Height / proportions** | 2.8 m when upright; very thin, with limbs 1.5× human proportion. Weighs little in Europa's gravity (0.134 g). |
| **Silhouette (instantly recognisable)** | A tall, narrow body topped not by a head but by a **vertical crest**: a fan of fine translucent filaments rising like a raised fin. When it listens, the crest flares wide. No face. |
| **Body structure** | Two long forelimbs ending in flat, wide **palms** (sensory pads) and hooked digits; two digitigrade hind limbs. Spine has six pressure nodes in a line. Ribbed torso that expands and contracts slowly, like breathing without lungs. |
| **Movement** | Low-gravity locomotion: long, slow bounding strides, then sudden flat-out acceleration on all fours. It doesn't run; it *falls forward* repeatedly. Joints occasionally hyper-extend, then settle, which looks unnatural but plausible. |
| **Sensory organs** | Senses **vibration** through its palms and crest. Outdoors (vacuum) it hunts by contact with the ice; indoors it also hears airborne sound. It is **almost blind**: it senses light and heat only at short range. |
| **Signature idle** | **Kneels with both palms flat on the ice, crest raised, motionless.** It is listening for you. If the player is moving, the crest twitches toward them. |
| **Detection behaviour** | Footsteps, gunfire, dropped objects and running create vibration events. Crouch-walking produces little. Standing still produces nothing. |
| **Attacks** | (1) Lunge-and-pin: palms slam the target down. (2) Crest strike: filaments harden and whip. (3) Grab-and-drag into a crack or vent. |
| **Audio** | Indoors: a dry, layered clicking from the crest; a deep, wet "pressure breath"; a rising **tonal hum that matches Feature 7's pitch** right before an attack. Outdoors: silent, but the suit picks up its footfalls through the ice as dull thuds. |
| **Weaknesses** | Rupturing **pressure nodes** (bright cyan, spine and shoulders), extreme cold exposure *after* the hide is breached, and loud decoy vibrations. |
| **Difficult to kill** | Body hits slow it and make it vent and stagger, but it **reseals**. After enough damage it collapses into **torpor**, which looks like death. It only truly dies if its nodes rupture *or* it is broken apart while in torpor. **This is the rule the player and Mara don't know in Chapter 8**, which is why it gets back up. Hana's notebook (found in Ch. 9) explains it, and the final escape lets the player use it. |
| **Death behaviour** | Nodes burst one after another; it deflates in stages; the crest collapses; the fluids freeze into a black crystalline shell. |
| **Environmental interaction** | Pushes through ice crust, crawls along ceilings indoors in low g, presses against doors to "listen" (you see its palms through the frosted window). |

### 8.2 Needles — fast, confined-space form

Eel-like, 1.2 m, eight short paired limbs, a body that compresses to fit through vents and cracks. They travel in **pairs**, and their pattern is always one distracts while one flanks. Individually weak (one or two hits). Audio: rapid ticking in vents, which works as an *early warning* inside pressurised spaces. Introduced in Chapter 7 so the player learns that vents are not safe.

### 8.3 The Keel — large form (set-piece only)

A massive, slow organism (~12 m), a living plough that moves *under* and *through* the ice shell to seal breaches. It is never fought, only fled. It appears during the Chapter 9 surface escape as a moving ridge of ice that cracks open and closes behind the player. Players mainly see it as a **rising ridge, a shadow under translucent ice, and once, briefly, its back**. Outdoors, the player hears it only through the boots: a huge, sub-bass grinding.

### 8.4 Rime — environmental organism

A white filament mat that **looks exactly like hoarfrost**. It is the colony's nervous tissue at the surface, a distributed ear. When sound or vibration happens near it, it **flickers with cyan light**, alerting nearby forms. Stealth gameplay: the player learns to read Rime as an alarm system and to move around it or distract it. Chapter 5 shows it as "frost in the wrong place" long before the player understands what it is.
